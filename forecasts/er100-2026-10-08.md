# Pre-commitment: interpretation of the ER-100 interim data (8 October 2026)

**Frozen:** 2 October 2026, 16:10 UTC — before any data are presented.  
**Framework:** The Saka Law v0.3.2 ([DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23092958](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23092958)), Sections 8.2 (EMS), 10 (LTI) and 12 (arrival hazard).  
**Status:** this is a small, informal pre-commitment, not the main preregistration of predictions P1–P8. Its purpose is to fix, in advance, how one event will be read, so that the interpretation cannot be adjusted after the data are seen. Any later edit to this file is visible in the git history; edits after the freeze time above do not count.

## 1. The event

Life Biosciences will present interim first-in-human data from its Phase 1 trial of ER-100 at Eyecelerator 2026 (New Orleans), on **8 October 2026, 14:10 CT**, presented by its Chief Scientific Officer.

Trial facts, from the ClinicalTrials.gov record **NCT07290244** as read on 2 October 2026:

| Item | Registered design |
|---|---|
| Intervention | AAV vector delivering OCT4, SOX2, KLF4 (OSK), intravitreal injection into **one eye**; expression switched on by oral doxycycline for 56 days |
| Population | Adults 40–85 with open-angle glaucoma (OAG) or non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION) |
| Design | Phase 1, non-randomized, sequential cohorts, **no masking**, no placebo arm |
| Cohorts | OAG low dose (2×10¹¹ vg/eye) → OAG high dose (6×10¹¹ vg/eye) → NAION at a selected dose (3, expandable to 6) |
| Enrollment | 18 (estimated) |
| Primary outcomes | Safety: adverse events, dose-limiting toxicities, laboratory tests, and safety readings of eye pressure, visual acuity, visual field, pERG, contrast sensitivity, OCT and slit lamp, to Day 56 and Day 112 |
| Secondary outcomes | The same visual measures read as efficacy; immune response to AAV2 and to ER-100; vector shedding; follow-up to 5 years |
| Dates | Start 2 March 2026; first participant dosed 9 June 2026; primary completion May 2027 (estimated) |

Since the first participant was dosed on 9 June, only participants dosed in June can have reached Day 112 by 8 October. The data will therefore be early and small.

## 3. How the result will be read in the framework

**Fixed regardless of the outcome:**

- The LTI level of partial epigenetic reprogramming stays at **L4 (Phase I)**. It moves to L5 only when a Phase 2 trial starts.
- The evidence is an **uncontrolled, unmasked first-in-human safety study**. On the EMS scale it sits between "human observational" (0.40) and "small controlled human trial" (0.55), and is recorded as **0.45** whatever the visual readings show.
- No change is made to any FTAF component or to predictions P1–P8. A single Phase 1 readout is an input to the readiness factor $G_j$ for this intervention class only.

**Direction of the update to $G_j$ (partial epigenetic reprogramming):**

| Outcome | Reading | Update to $G_j$ |
|---|---|---|
| A. No DLTs, no serious treatment-related AEs, inflammation absent or mild and manageable | Expected for a Phase 1 that proceeds; the main risk of the modality (uncontrolled expression of reprogramming factors) has not appeared in the first months | Small increase |
| B. As A, **plus** improvement in the treated eye that exceeds the noise thresholds below, consistent across at least one functional and one structural measure, in most participants, and larger than in the fellow eye | First human signal of functional effect; still unmasked, uncontrolled and very small | Moderate increase |
| C. As A, plus improvements below the noise thresholds or in a single measure only | Indistinguishable from test–retest variability, learning effects and selective reporting | Same as A |
| D. A DLT, a serious treatment-related AE, severe inflammation, or any sign of abnormal growth | The modality's central safety risk is not excluded | Decrease; size depends on severity and on whether dosing continues |
| E. Presentation gives no participant-level or numerical data | No information beyond "the trial continues" | No change |

**Noise thresholds fixed in advance.** Glaucoma progresses slowly and these tests are noisy, so per-participant changes smaller than the following are treated as noise:

- Best-corrected visual acuity: less than **5 ETDRS letters** (one chart line).
- Humphrey visual field mean deviation: less than **2 dB**.
- OCT ganglion-cell or nerve-fibre layer thickness: any **increase** would be unusual in glaucoma and is noted, but is not counted as a functional benefit on its own.

Because neither participants nor assessors are masked, functional tests that depend on the participant's response (acuity, visual field, contrast sensitivity) carry less weight than objective ones (pERG, OCT).

## 4. A related claim, read with the same rules

On 1 October 2026 David Sinclair reported on X that his laboratory had presented, at ARDD 2026, a pilot study of a "v2" reprogramming approach in LAKI mice, with strong protection against frailty and an extension of maximum lifespan of more than 33%, with some treated mice still alive. He later described v2 as a drink given three times a week, with composition to be disclosed later.

Reading under the framework:

- **LTI level L2 (animal)**; EMS **0.20**, below the standard animal level of 0.25 because the result is a conference pilot, unpublished, with sample size not reported and composition not disclosed.
- LAKI mice carry a progeria mutation and age abnormally fast. A lifespan effect in this model is not evidence about normal ageing; a similar effect was already reported for partial reprogramming in the same model in 2016 (Ocampo et al., *Cell* 167, 1719–1733).
- Maximum lifespan cannot be computed while treated animals are still alive.
- **No update to $G_j$** until at least (a) a preprint or paper with sample sizes and complete survival curves, (b) the composition is disclosed, and (c) an effect is shown in naturally aged wild-type mice.
